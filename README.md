Key business insight
Order demand is flat, while cancellations and unresolved order statuses offer clearer improvement opportunities than simply increasing marketing spend.

The analysis covers 8,000 orders from 2,775 customers, dated January 1, 2024–December 31, 2025.

What the business should act on
1. Investigate mobile-app cancellations.
The app generates 37.9% of orders, but its cancellation rate is 5.64%, compared with 4.08% on the website—a 1.56-percentage-point gap.

Investigate payment failures, checkout experience, stock availability, and cancellation reasons.
This is an observed association, not proof that the app causes cancellations. Do not reallocate marketing budget on this evidence alone.
2. Reconcile unresolved order statuses.
There are 543 Processing and 780 Shipped orders. Confirm whether these represent genuine outstanding work or stale records before using delivery rates as an operational KPI.

The file has no status-update timestamps or delivery dates, so these orders cannot be classified as overdue.
3. Plan inventory and campaigns around the observed Q4 uplift.
October–December accounts for 30.4% of orders, despite representing roughly one-quarter of the year. Q4 is stronger in both years, supporting advance capacity planning—but two years are insufficient to establish a reliable seasonal forecast.

4. Test repeat-purchase marketing, while measuring it properly.
Of customers observed in 2024, 73.5% also ordered in 2025. This supports testing replenishment reminders, loyalty offers, or personalized follow-ups.

This is observed customer return, not acquisition-cohort retention; customers’ histories before 2024 are unknown.
Measure incremental results with a holdout group rather than assuming repeat orders were driven by marketing.
